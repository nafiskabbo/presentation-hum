# Presentation Requirements: Vision-Guided Drone Food Delivery

## 1. Purpose

This file is the single source of truth for the group presentation deck. Read this whole
file before generating anything. Do not start work on a section until you have read the
rules for that section below.

Business idea under analysis: **Vision-Guided Drone Food Delivery**, a service where
computer vision lets drones fly a fixed street level corridor from a restaurant hub to a
customer's balcony or rooftop landing pad in dense urban Bangladesh, mostly Dhaka.

The deck is a **humanities (business and management) course presentation**. It is judged
on business reasoning quality, not on software engineering content. Every section must use
a named business framework and make an argument, not just list facts.

## 2. Business context and future scope

- Primary market: Dhaka, then Chattogram, Sylhet and Rajshahi as corridors mature.
- Primary problem being solved: road congestion makes rider delivery slow and expensive,
  orders get cancelled, hot food arrives cold, and rider supply is thin.
- Current scope: food delivery from partner restaurants to customers.
- Stated future scope, mentioned but not built out in detail: pharma and medical product
  delivery, including cold chain for insulin and vaccines, plus blood and diagnostic sample
  movement. Treat this as the Section 8 expansion story and as one line of Section 2 revenue.
- Positioning line to reuse: the drone is not a faster rider, it is a bypass of the road.

### Section 1: Business Idea and Market Problem (Srijon, 2303179)

Must cover:
- What the service is, in one sentence a non technical audience can repeat.
- The step by step operational loop: order received, kitchen prep, drone loaded at hub,
  corridor flight, computer vision descent, landing, customer collects, drone returns.
- The specific customer problem solved, with a Dhaka scenario, not generic statements.
- Target customer segments: dense high rise neighbourhoods, students and young
  professionals, time sensitive orders, restaurants that lose orders during peak.
- The Value Proposition Canvas: customer jobs, pains, gains, pain relievers, gain creators,
  and the three blocks of the business model (products, services, revenue).

Required visuals: one end to end service flow diagram, one value proposition canvas layout,
one map style corridor diagram of Dhaka with hub and landing pad nodes.

### Section 2: Business Model and Revenue Strategy (Nafis Islam Kabbo, 2303180)

Must cover:
- Full Business Model Canvas, all nine blocks, mapped to the actual drone food service.
- Revenue streams, each with a rough price logic: per delivery fee, restaurant commission
  percentage, monthly subscription tiers, sponsored or boosted listings, enterprise and
  white label contracts, and the later pharma and cold chain contract.
- Operating cost structure: drone hardware, battery and charging, pilot and hub staff,
  software and computer vision engineering, maintenance, insurance, landing pad and hub
  infrastructure, regulatory and compliance cost, customer support.
- Unit economics: cost per delivery, contribution margin per delivery, and how many
  deliveries per day a hub needs to break even.
- A clear statement of why the fee can be higher than a rider fee and still be accepted.

Required visuals: a nine block Business Model Canvas, a revenue mix doughnut chart, a cost
stack bar or waterfall, and a small unit economics table.

### Section 3: Internal Strengths and Weaknesses (Mubashshirul Haque, 2303127)

Strengths, all internal to the firm and defensible: delivery time independent of traffic,
automation of the last stretch, no rider commission per delivery, consistent and repeatable
service, ability to serve areas with poor road access, lower fuel exposure, dense data
collection for routing, brand differentiation.

Weaknesses, internal: battery range and payload limits, weather and monsoon exposure,
capital heavy start, dependence on a small engineering team, limited fleet size, need for
physical landing pad infrastructure, unproven at scale in Bangladesh, high battery
replacement cost, night flying restrictions.

Required visuals: a two column strengths and weaknesses layout with weight indicators, and
an honest weaknesses mitigation column. Do not present strengths as a marketing list, each
strength should be paired with the weakness it cannot solve.

### Section 4: External Opportunities and Threats (Refayet, 2303148)

Opportunities, external: growing food delivery demand, rapid urbanisation and vertical
housing, falling drone and battery cost curves, improving computer vision maturity,
government smart city and digital economy push, logistics and pharma cold chain needs,
ride hailing and gig platforms as talent pools, drone tech grants and incubators.

Threats, external: DGCA and CAAB regulatory uncertainty and possible bans, air safety and
accident liability, public and resident resistance and noise complaints, established food
delivery platforms copying or blocking the idea, cheap rider labour keeping costs low,
security and misuse concerns, monsoon and cyclonic weather, insurance and legal costs.

Required visuals: a two column opportunities and threats layout, and a small
impact versus likelihood positioning scatter for the top five threats.

### Section 5: External Business Environment (Nafis Sadot, 2303174)

PESTEL, one clear block per factor, with an implication for the business in each block:
- Political: national digital economy policy, CAAB airspace and drone licensing, local
  city corporation permissions, import duty and procurement rules.
- Economic: exchange rate and imported parts cost, inflation and food prices, funding
  environment, fuel and electricity cost, delivery willingness to pay.
- Social: traffic behaviour, rooftop culture in Dhaka, noise sensitivity, food delivery
  habit, trust in unmanned aircraft, elderly and disability access.
- Technological: computer vision maturity, 5G and broadband coverage, battery energy
  density, local hardware fabrication, cloud and mapping data availability.
- Environmental: monsoon, humidity, heat, air quality, building density causing airspace
  congestion, crash debris risk, noise.
- Legal: aviation law, data protection for camera feeds, liability for property damage,
  no fly zones around state and defence locations, food safety liability.

Required visuals: a six cell PESTEL grid, plus a small "factor versus impact" ranking bar
showing which two factors matter most.

### Section 6: Competition and Industry Analysis (Neloy Nandi, 2303177)

Must cover:
- Incumbent food delivery platforms in Bangladesh and how their model and cost structure
  differ from a drone corridor model.
- Traditional rider delivery as the real substitute, and rider economics.
- Other drone delivery attempts and drone logistics companies anywhere in the world, what
  they proved, and why their model does or does not port to Dhaka.
- Porter's Five Forces: rivalry among existing competitors, threat of new entrants,
  supplier power, buyer power, and threat of substitutes. Each force gets a strength
  rating with a one line justification.
- Barriers to entry: capital, regulatory approval, engineering talent, landing pad
  network, and data.
- Supplier power: drone and battery OEMs, flight software, cloud and mapping providers,
  restaurant partners. Buyer power: customers, and restaurant partners who can multi home.

Required visuals: a competitor comparison matrix, a Five Forces diagram with intensity
ratings, and a barriers to entry funnel.

### Section 7: Operations, People and Risk Management (Anindo, 2303181)

Must cover:
- The operating value chain from order to return, with a hand off table showing owner,
  time and cost per step.
- People and roles: hub manager, drone pilot, computer vision engineer, flight software
  engineer, backend and dispatch engineer, battery and airframe technician, restaurant
  partner staff, customer support, and the future regulatory liaison role.
- Fleet operations: pre flight checks, battery swap and charging rotation, maintenance
  intervals, spare airframes, landing pad maintenance, weather go no go rules, airspace
  corridor design, backup landing and recall procedure.
- Navigation and landing safety specifics: fiducial or marker based landing, geofencing,
  obstacle detection and avoidance, human detection and abort, redundant positioning,
  and what the computer vision system actually sees.
- Cybersecurity: camera feed privacy, GPS spoofing and jamming, remote takeover and
  geofence enforcement, credential security, and fleet control system availability.
- A formal risk register table: risk, cause, probability, impact, severity, mitigation,
  owner. Minimum eight risks.
- Human risk: falling objects, pedestrian exposure at landing, food safety and temperature,
  noise, and worker safety for maintenance staff.

Required visuals: an operations flow with owners, a risk matrix heat map, the risk register
table, and an org style role card layout.

### Section 8: Social Impact, Ethics and Future Strategy (Jebon, 2303160)

Must cover:
- Triple Bottom Line, treated as an honest assessment rather than a victory lap.
  - People: rider displacement in the short run, rider retraining into hub and maintenance
    roles, new technical jobs, faster access to food, and improved access for elderly and
    mobility limited customers.
  - Planet: comparison of delivery footprint against petrol or electric two wheelers,
    added manufacturing and energy footprint, battery end of life, and the conditions under
    which the drone case is genuinely greener.
  - Profit: margin profile, the cash position needed to survive the first years, and why
    the model only works at hub density.
- Ethics: camera privacy and no facial recognition commitment, data retention, noise
  reduction design, informed community consent, affordability and no surge pricing for
  vulnerable customers, transparent incident reporting, and fairness to displaced riders.
- Future strategy in three horizons: horizon 1 food density in Dhaka, horizon 2 pharma,
  cold chain and blood logistics, horizon 3 disaster response, rural last mile, and regional
  export of the operating model.
- Public acceptance programme: pilot with one campus, community noise and safety reporting,
  and a published incident log.
- Closing verdict: is this business viable in Bangladesh, and under what conditions.

Required visuals: a Triple Bottom Line three column layout, a three horizon roadmap, and a
conditions of success table.

## 4. Facts, numbers and citations

- Every statistic, market size, cost, price or claim must be traceable to a real, citable
  source. Sources must include BTRC, CAAB, the Bangladesh Bureau of Statistics, Dhaka
  Metropolitan Police traffic data, Uber and Careem published rider figures, foodpanda
  and Pathao public statements, DG CAAM and DGCA drone guidance, published drone delivery
  case studies, and peer reviewed or industry research on urban air mobility.
- No invented sources. Never write a citation to a document you have not confirmed exists.
- If a number is a planning assumption rather than a sourced fact, label it on the slide
  as **Model assumption** and keep assumptions in a clearly separated list.
- Cite on the slide where the claim appears, in short form such as (BTRC, 2024).
- Include a final references slide in IEEE format. Do not display raw URLs in the deck; the
  references slide may carry DOIs or publisher names only.
- If a required figure cannot be sourced, state the qualitative point instead of inventing
  a number.

## 7. Deck and design requirements

- Light, primarily white background. Academic, not corporate startup.
- Palette, restrained and consistent:
  - Ink and graphite `#0B1220` for headings and body text
  - Rotor teal `#0F4C5C` as the primary brand and structural accent
  - Warm amber `#D97706` for emphasis, highlights and revenue related figures
  - Signal red `#B91C1C` reserved for threats, risks and weaknesses
  - Leaf green `#15803D` reserved for opportunities and strengths
  - Neutral border `#E2E8F0` for table rules and dividers
- Use colour semantically, not decoratively. A colour must mean the same thing on every
  slide.
- Typography: Century Schoolbook for headers, Calibri for body and chart labels. Strict
  hierarchy, consistent margins and a real grid. No more than two type sizes per slide.
- Diagrams, native charts, flowcharts, matrices and tables carry the argument. Text
  density must stay low. No paragraph longer than three lines anywhere.
- Every framework slide must be a purpose built diagram, not a bulleted list pretending to
  be a framework.
- Show the current section topic on every slide so the audience always knows where they are.
- Footer on every slide with the presentation title on the left.
- Zero padded slide counter in `current/total` format, for example `01/34`, bottom right.
  The total must equal the real slide count.
- First slide shows the title, the group name, and all eight student IDs with names.
- No em dashes or en dashes anywhere in slides or speech files. Use commas, colons or
  periods.
- No raw URLs in the deck body.
- The Thank You and Discussion slide must be minimal, elegant and traditional: a short
  closing line, the group name, and a single clear invitation for questions.
- The deck must be readable on a projector: minimum body size 16 pt, no important text
  below 12 pt, and no more than one visual claim per slide.

## 8. Media, animation and motion graphics

Native PowerPoint animation timelines are not reliably writable with the current toolchain.
Deliver motion in these ways instead, and state which method was used per slide:

1. **Motion video inserts**: short looping or one shot MP4 motion pieces embedded as
   video, with a poster frame so the slide still reads if video fails.
2. **Motion style animation via image sequences**: animated diagrams built as frame based
   images placed with the deck's add image tooling, only where a short build of 3 to 6
   frames is enough to explain a sequence.
3. **Consistent static builds**: reveal complexity by stepping slide to slide with matched
   layouts, which is how most of the deck should work.
4. **Simple OOXML transitions**: only slide level fades, and only if the pptx skill
   validator passes afterwards. Report the result honestly if not.

Rules for media:

- Never use stock footage of a real identifiable person, real military or police imagery,
  or any clip with visible foreign military branding.
- Fictional, non branded drones only. No real company logos, no real drone product names
  that imply endorsement, no identifiable rider faces without a written consent note.
- Keep the deck file size reasonable. Compress inserts, prefer short loops, and keep total
  embedded media under about 40 MB.
- Every media asset must be listed in `media/motion_brief.md` with slide number, purpose,
  duration, loop count, and how it was produced.
- If a needed asset cannot be produced in this environment, still write the prompt and
  place a clean labelled placeholder frame on the slide. Never leave an unexplained gap.

### Image and video prompt hand off

Write `media/asset_prompt_sheet.md` and give the user the prompts directly in chat, so
nothing depends on a tool being available. For each asset include:

- Asset ID and slide number
- Purpose in the argument
- Type: still image, motion loop, or video clip
- Aspect ratio, resolution, and duration for video
- Style keywords consistent with the deck palette and light background
- Full prompt text
- Negative prompt text
- Safe area note, so text can be placed over it
- Priority: must have, nice to have

Cover at minimum: title background, service flow, Dhaka corridor map, drone exterior,
landing pad close up, hub interior, competitor matrix support art, risk matrix
supporting art, and a closing background. Aim for roughly 12 to 18 assets.

## 9. Speech script requirements

- One file: `speeches/full_presentation_speech.md`, in presenter order, each section
  headed with the presenter name, student ID, and the slide range.
- Target 2.5 to 3.5 minutes per presenter, 30 to 45 seconds per slide, roughly 20 to 25
  minutes total for the deck.
- For each slide give: the slide number, a delivery cue, the spoken script, emphasis
  words, and a timing note. Add a handover line at every section boundary.
- Tone: professional, confident and a little witty for a university audience. Relatable
  Dhaka hooks work well, for example the rider who knows a shortcut the map does not, or
  the neighbour who complains about a drone before the food even arrives. Keep the humour
  to one beat per presenter.
- Connect to local reality: mention specific Dhaka conditions such as monsoon, rooftop
  access, narrow streets, the rider labour market, and traffic. Do not lecture.
- Numbers spoken aloud must match the slide. Do not read bullet points aloud verbatim.
- Add a short optional Bangla one line cue per section, since the audience is local.
  Mark it clearly as optional and keep it to a single line.
- No em dashes anywhere.
- For Section 7, the risk mitigation explanation must be concrete enough that a
  classmate can repeat it. For Section 8, the Triple Bottom Line must be honest about the
  negative cases, not a sales pitch.
